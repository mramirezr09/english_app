In English, we use different persons. I, you, he, she, it, we and they.
The action to B changes depending on the person.
For example, with I, we use M.
I am a teacher.
With you, we use R.
You are a student.
And with he, she and it, we use is.
He is my brother.
She is my friend.
It is a nice day.
Finally, with we and they, we use R.
We are in the classroom.
They are nice people.
Pretty simple, right?
Now let's look at the functions of the action to B.
There are four basic functions.
First, we use the action to B for names.
For example, he is Sam.
She is Patty.
We also use to B for descriptions and feelings.
Sam is sad.
Patty is happy.
Another common function is professions.
I am a doctor.
You are a teacher.
And finally, we use to B for locations.
They are at home.
We are in Canada.
Now let's learn the negative form of the action to B.
To express a negative idea, we add the word not after the action to B.
Look at this example.
He is happy.
This idea is affirmative.
To make it negative, we add the word not after the action to B.
He is not happy.
Let's look at more examples.
They are not in Canada.
They are in Mexico.
We are not sad.
We are happy.
